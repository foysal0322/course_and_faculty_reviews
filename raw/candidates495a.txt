   - CSE440: Artificial Intelligence / This course is mostly theory-based and provides a broad introduction to AI. It doesn’t offer much specialization in any specific topic, but it’s still a good choice if you’re looking for an elective or open elective that can help boost your CGPA. / I took it under MSRb sir. He mainly followed Stanford’s AI Course (CS221). Four modules are covered: Reflex-based Models, State-based Models, Variable-based Models, and Logic-based Models. The structure was organized and easy to follow, especially if you’re new to AI concepts. / The grading system included 2 quizzes (best one counted), a bonus viva on quiz day, a midterm that was more problem-solving and math-oriented, a final exam that combined theory with math, and one group project assigned by Sir. Overall, it’s a manageable and helpful course if you want a solid overview of AI without diving too deep.
   - CSE445: Machine Learning / This is a traditional ML course that helps you build a solid foundation in the field. It mainly focuses on regression, classification algorithms, and evaluation metrics. Prerequisites include Python, Statistics, and LaTeX, so having a basic grip on these is important. / I took it under MAQm sir. His lectures were mostly based on Aurélien Géron’s ML textbook and StatQuest with Josh Starmer. Topics covered throughout the semester included EDA, preprocessing, regression, classification, SVM, decision trees, ensemble learning, unsupervised learning, reinforcement learning, and even a short intro to neural networks. / The course had one surprise quiz, one coding-based ML assignment with a report and viva, a mid and final exam (both allowing a one-sided cheat sheet), and a group project. The question patterns are MCQs, mathematical, and short questions. The project carried significant weight with marks for proposal, progress, final report, demo, and viva. Project Evaluation is done by the Sir, TA and RA. Overall, your grade will depend a lot on the assignment and project, so those are the key parts to focus on. Overall, this course provides a good understanding of ML at the beginner level.

   - The course introduces basic robotics concepts starting with motion models like the unicycle and differential drive, explaining how robots move and are controlled using open-loop and closed-loop systems( sir also gave simulation assignments) . Besides that motion planning techniques such as PRM, RRT, and A* algorithms are covered which help robots navigate around obstacles. Lyapunov’s method is taught to ensure the robot’s stability. The course also explains camera projection models such as the thin lens , perspective projection, / for calibrating robot vision. Image processing techniques like smoothing and edge detection are introduced to help robots interpret visual data. SLAM is covered to show how robots build maps and locate themselves in unknown environments. To handle noisy sensor data, methods like line fitting using split-and-merge and RANSAC are taught, allowing robots to extract reliable information and correct errors. These tools form the foundation of practical robot perception and control. It was a very interesting and fun course. Although a single semester isn't enough to learn about robotics. On the other hand, Sns1 sir is very accomplished ,friendly, helpful and humble person. It was a great learning experience!
