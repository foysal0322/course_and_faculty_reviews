   - CSE440: Artificial Intelligence / This course is mostly theory-based and provides a broad introduction to AI. It doesn’t offer much specialization in any specific topic, but it’s still a good choice if you’re looking for an elective or open elective that can help boost your CGPA. / I took it under MSRb sir. He mainly followed Stanford’s AI Course (CS221). Four modules are covered: Reflex-based Models, State-based Models, Variable-based Models, and Logic-based Models. The structure was organized and easy to follow, especially if you’re new to AI concepts. / The grading system included 2 quizzes (best one counted), a bonus viva on quiz day, a midterm that was more problem-solving and math-oriented, a final exam that combined theory with math, and one group project assigned by Sir. Overall, it’s a manageable and helpful course if you want a solid overview of AI without diving too deep.
   - CSE445: Machine Learning / This is a traditional ML course that helps you build a solid foundation in the field. It mainly focuses on regression, classification algorithms, and evaluation metrics. Prerequisites include Python, Statistics, and LaTeX, so having a basic grip on these is important. / I took it under MAQm sir. His lectures were mostly based on Aurélien Géron’s ML textbook and StatQuest with Josh Starmer. Topics covered throughout the semester included EDA, preprocessing, regression, classification, SVM, decision trees, ensemble learning, unsupervised learning, reinforcement learning, and even a short intro to neural networks. / The course had one surprise quiz, one coding-based ML assignment with a report and viva, a mid and final exam (both allowing a one-sided cheat sheet), and a group project. The question patterns are MCQs, mathematical, and short questions. The project carried significant weight with marks for proposal, progress, final report, demo, and viva. Project Evaluation is done by the Sir, TA and RA. Overall, your grade will depend a lot on the assignment and project, so those are the key parts to focus on. Overall, this course provides a good understanding of ML at the beginner level.

   - CSE465: Pattern Recognition & Neural Network / This is a good specialized course in the AI trail, giving you a solid understanding of Deep Learning. Although Machine Learning is not a prerequisite in the curriculum, having prior knowledge of ML definitely helps in understanding deep learning topics more easily. Prerequisites include Python, Statistics, LaTeX, and ideally some ML background. / I took it under SvA Ma’am. She followed the 100 Days of Deep Learning YouTube playlist for lectures. Throughout the semester, she covered three main architectures: Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN). In addition, the TA conducted two coding sessions on PyTorch. / The evaluation system included two quizzes (best one counted), one midterm, and one final exam. Most questions were based on the specific architectures, combining theory with mathematics. There were no assignments in this course. For the group project, the TA provided three domains, and students could pick one to propose their project idea. About two weeks were given for the project, so starting early is highly recommended. The project was evaluated by both the Maam and the TA. Overall, this course helps you gain specialization in Deep Learning, making it a valuable continuation after the Machine Learning course.

   - Shafayat Osman Sir, what is your opinion about how a student should set up their electives or what should be the general goto in choosing electives without a plan being narrowed down ? Thank you
   - Jarif Mohsin In my opinion, electives should be used to explore new fields of interest. Let’s say you are in your third or fourth year and you still don’t know which field of CS you want to work on or pursue in future. Electives can be a major help here. Take electives from different trails like ML or Networking or Software Engineering and see which of these fields you resonate with the most. These courses can serve as the gateway to your future field of expertise. Alternatively, you can also use electives as a means to dip your toe in a new field if you are already well-versed in an area. For example, my prime research interest is Cybersecurity and Computer Networks. But during my undergrad, I did my electives from ML and Theoretical Computer Science to learn about new things since I was well versed in CyberSec and Networking through my own self-study. / To sum up: use the electives to either see which field you want to work on or to see what other fields look like if you already have an expertise.
